§1 — Your Founder Archetype

The cross-force pattern that names what you are

THE AD-FUNDED FOUNDER
The founder who scaled the revenue by buying it — running paid ads to attract customers, but never doing the work of figuring out what makes them stay.

The classic version of this pattern is the small clinic owner spending ₹80,000/month on Google Ads with a landing page that says "Quality healthcare for the family." Zero results. They believe they have a marketing problem. They actually have a position problem wearing a marketing-spend costume.

You are running the same pattern at 30 times the size.

You've done what most founders running this pattern never manage: you scaled the ad spend, built a 22-person team to absorb the work, and crossed ₹25 Cr in revenue. On the outside it looks like a real company. But the underlying signals in your own answers are identical to the small clinic:

  • Q42 How marketing actually runs: You picked "Layer-3 Bleeder — ads with no clear message."
  • Q39 Where the marketing money goes: Ads, reels, agencies, hoardings.
  • Q43 Positioning chosen and defended: None picked.
  • Q37 Two-word position: Vague — "quality, trusted, premium, authentic."
  • Q44 Why customers come: Not really word-of-mouth.
  • Q2 Why customers pick you: "I haven't asked, but I'm pretty sure it's price, service, or quality."

You even told us where the real leverage is. On Qx2 — the painful, high-value lever — you picked: "Way 3 — more frequent transactions (repeat customers)." Then on Q67 — what you've actually worked on this year — you picked: "Only Way 1 (more clients) — chasing leads."

You know the answer. You're working on the comfortable lever (more leads) and avoiding the painful one (keeping the customers you already have). That's the pattern. Every new lead pushes back the harder conversation, which is why are my existing customers leaving?

The move this pattern always reaches for

"Better ads. Better creative. Better agency. Better funnel."

It's never the right answer. It's a marketing-spend fix to a position hole.

§4 — Your Immediate Move (the 90-Day Sprint)

One sprint. One number. One sentence.

THE CUSTOMER-VOICE SPRINT
In the next 90 days, get one sentence — in your customers' exact words — explaining why they bought you over the cheaper alternative they were also considering.

Target: 30 customer interviews completed → 5 verbatim sentences written down → 1 chosen as the position you will defend for the next 12 months.

Why this move, not another

Your weakest force is X-Factor (0.9), and that weakness sits underneath every other force's weakness. You can't fix marketing until you know what to say. You can't fix sales conversion until you know what to defend. You can't fix financial discipline until you can charge a real price for a real position. A position written without customer evidence is a position that won't survive the first time a prospect pushes back on price.

You need raw material. Your existing customers are the only people who have it.

The 4-Week Plan

1 — Set upPull the list of every customer who bought in the last 18 months. Score by recency + engagement. Pick 30. Send the call request personally — no team, no template, your name in the subject line.Don't review ad creative this week. Don't start new agency conversations.

The Stop-Doing List (specific to your answers)

These are five things to actively stop — not "deprioritise," not "later" — for the full 90 days:

  1. Stop spending the next ₹50L of marketing budget on new ads. Redirect it to: (a) the 30 calls (cost: near-zero), (b) the cost of redoing landing pages once you have the sentence (₹2-3L), (c) reserve. Anything left over becomes the defence fund for the new position. [Q17]
  2. Stop attending sales calls where you talk more than 50% of the time. Set a timer. If you cross 50%, end the call. [Q49]
  3. Stop saying "the economy" or "the timing" out loud about lost deals. Replace with one of: "they wanted a guarantee I don't offer," "I didn't ask them what they were comparing me to," "I talked instead of listening." [Q36, Q51]
  4. Stop hiring anyone new — including the next agency, coach-trainer, ops person — for the full 90 days. With 22 people and no named bottleneck (Q64), the next hire makes the disorganisation worse, not better. [Q12, Q64]
  5. Stop checking WhatsApp / email / Instagram in the first 60 minutes of waking. First action of the day is one 30-minute block on the Customer-Voice Sprint. Phone after that. [Q85, Q88]

Your first concrete action

Open your CRM. Pick the three customers who bought most recently AND are still active.

Send each one a calendar link for a 30-minute "I'd love your honest take" call. Use your own name, your own phone — not your assistant, not a Calendly email blast.

You can do this in the next 20 minutes. If you have not booked three calls by Friday night of the week you read this report, the rest of the sprint will not happen.

Appendix B

Glossary

The 8 Forces

The eight depth dimensions of the bootcamp curriculum.

  • Identity (1): who you serve and what business you're actually in.
  • X-Factor (2): the cost you pay that competitors refuse to.
  • Marketing (3): the position, the message, the channel.
  • Sales (4): turning interest into a paying customer.
  • Financial (5): the honesty of your numbers.
  • Optimisation (6): working through the real bottleneck, not around it.
  • Scale (7): the asset that exists without you.
  • Owner Energy (8): what you've subtracted to make the important few possible.

The 9 Lies

Common founder beliefs the bootcamp surfaces and breaks. Identity Trap, X-Factor Hunt, Marketing Lie, Sales Lie, Conversion Lie, Profit Lie, Growth Lie, Freedom Lie, Death Spiral.

The 7 Founder Archetypes

Cross-force patterns common to Indian SME founders. The Grinder, The Inheritor, The Migrator (here called The Ad-Funded Founder for plain language), The Name (The Personal Brand), The Rainmaker's Firm (The Agency Model), The Institution, The Architect.

Replacement wage (Bus Test)

What the market would pay to do your job, end-to-end. Used to correct the real margin — true profit = stated profit minus the implicit subsidy of paying yourself less than market.

Layer-3 Bleeder

Running paid distribution (ads, reels, agencies) without a clear position underneath. The single most expensive recoverable mistake in Indian SME marketing.

Customer-Voice Sprint

A 90-day instrument — 30 customer interviews → 5 verbatim sentences → 1 chosen position. The rule: a position written without customer evidence won't survive its first pricing objection.

Methodology

Three-step pipeline. (1) A scoring engine computes your 8 force depths from your 96 answers via a published rubric. (2) Stage band + force fingerprint + specific question tiebreakers pick your archetype. (3) The Lie is picked from your weakest force and specific answer patterns.
