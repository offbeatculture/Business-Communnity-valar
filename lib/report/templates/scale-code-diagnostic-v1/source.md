## §1 — Your Founder Archetype

# THE AD-FUNDED FOUNDER

### The pattern in one sentence

> *The founder who scaled the revenue by buying it — running paid ads to attract customers, but never doing the work of figuring out what makes them stay.*

The classic version of this pattern is the small clinic owner spending ₹80,000/month on Google Ads with a landing page that says "Quality healthcare for the family." Zero results. They believe they have a marketing problem. They actually have a **position problem** wearing a marketing-spend costume.

You are running the **same pattern at 30 times the size**.

You've done what most founders running this pattern never manage: you scaled the ad spend, built a 22-person team to absorb the work, and crossed ₹25 Cr in revenue. On the outside it looks like a real company. But the underlying signals in your own answers are identical to the small clinic:

- **Q42 — How marketing actually runs:** You picked "Layer-3 Bleeder — ads with no clear message."
- **Q39 — Where the marketing money goes:** Ads, reels, agencies, hoardings.
- **Q43 — Positioning chosen and defended:** None picked.
- **Q37 — Two-word position:** Vague — "quality, trusted, premium, authentic."
- **Q44 — Why customers come:** Not really word-of-mouth.
- **Q2 — Why customers pick you:** "I haven't asked, but I'm pretty sure it's price, service, or quality."

You even told us where the real leverage is. **On Qx2 — the painful, high-value lever — you picked: "Way 3 — more frequent transactions (repeat customers)."** Then on Q67 — what you've actually worked on this year — you picked: **"Only Way 1 (more clients) — chasing leads."**

You know the answer. You're working on the comfortable lever (more leads) and avoiding the painful one (keeping the customers you already have). That's the pattern. Every new lead pushes back the harder conversation, which is *why are my existing customers leaving?*

### The move this pattern always reaches for

> *"Better ads. Better creative. Better agency. Better funnel."*

It's never the right answer. It's a marketing-spend fix to a position hole.

The X-Factor Hunt is a lie because while you're hunting for the answer inside your head, the people who would tell you for free — your existing customers — are sitting in your CRM, never asked. The cheapest answer in the business is the one you haven't gone to get.

There's a deeper cost too. The reason your conversion is under 10% and your "chasing leads" feels exhausting is that **you're pitching without knowing what to defend**. A sales call where you talk 70% of the time (Q49) on a generic offer ("price, service, quality") is your-voice filling the silence your customer's voice should have filled.

## §4 — Your Immediate Move (the 90-Day Sprint)

# THE CUSTOMER-VOICE SPRINT

> **In the next 90 days, get one sentence — in your customers' exact words — explaining why they bought you over the cheaper alternative they were also considering.**

**Target:** 30 customer interviews completed → 5 verbatim sentences written down → 1 chosen as the position you will defend for the next 12 months.

**Why this move, not another:** Your weakest force is X-Factor (0.9), and that weakness sits underneath every other force's weakness. You can't fix marketing until you know what to say. You can't fix sales conversion until you know what to defend. You can't fix financial discipline until you can charge a real price for a real position. **A position written without customer evidence is a position that won't survive the first time a prospect pushes back on price.**

You need raw material. Your existing customers are the only people who have it.

---

### The 4-Week Plan

| Week | What you do | What you stop doing |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Set up** | Pull the list of every customer who bought in the last 18 months. Score them by how recent + how engaged. Pick 30. Send the call request personally — no team, no template, your name in the subject line. | Don't review ad creative this week. Don't start any new agency conversations. |
| **2 — Listen** | Do 30 calls, 30 minutes each. Three questions only: (a) What were you doing before you bought from us? (b) What other options were you considering? (c) Why did you pick us? Record everything. | Don't attend any internal meeting that isn't about delivery. |
| **3 — Pattern-spot** | Pull the verbatim sentences. Cluster them. Find the 3-5 phrases customers actually say (not what you wish they said). | No "creative reviews" with the agency. They have nothing to optimise until Week 4. |
| **4 — Lock** | Pick ONE sentence. Re-brief the agency, the sales team, the landing pages. Set a 12-week defence period — no changes to position. Track whether conversion moves. | Stop *all* positioning experiments outside the chosen sentence. |

---

### The Stop-Doing List (specific to your answers)

These are five things to actively *stop* — not "deprioritise," not "later" — for the full 90 days:

1. **Stop spending the next ₹50L of marketing budget on new ads.** Redirect it to: (a) the 30 calls (cost: near-zero), (b) the cost of redoing landing pages once you have the sentence (₹2-3L), (c) reserve. Anything left over becomes the *defence fund* for the new position. [Q17]
2. **Stop attending sales calls where you talk more than 50% of the time.** Set a timer. If you cross 50%, end the call. [Q49]
3. **Stop saying "the economy" or "the timing" out loud about lost deals.** Replace with one of: "they wanted a guarantee I don't offer," "I didn't ask them what they were comparing me to," "I talked instead of listening." [Q36, Q51]
4. **Stop hiring anyone new — including the next agency, the next coach-trainer, the next ops person — for the full 90 days.** With 22 people and no named bottleneck (Q64), the next hire makes the disorganisation worse, not better. [Q12, Q64]
5. **Stop checking WhatsApp / email / Instagram in the first 60 minutes of waking.** First action of the day is one 30-minute block on the Customer-Voice Sprint. Phone after that. [Q85, Q88]

---

### Your first concrete action

Open your CRM. Pick the three customers who bought most recently AND are still active.

Send each one a calendar link for a 30-minute "I'd love your honest take" call. Use your own name, your own phone — not your assistant, not a Calendly email blast.

You can do this in the next 20 minutes.

If you have not booked three calls by Friday night of the week you read this report, the rest of the sprint will not happen.

## §8 — The Three Decisions This Report Helps You Make

### Decision 1 — Do I hire next, and for which role?

**Recommendation: don't hire anyone in the next 90 days.**

Your bottleneck (Q64) is not yet named. Your 22-person team is operating without a clear operating system underneath it (Q70). Hiring person #23 right now would compound the disorganisation, not fix it.

If you're pressed on *which* role would be the right one when you're ready — it's a **Head of Marketing whose first 90 days are the Customer-Voice Sprint plus rebuilding the position**. Not a marketing executor. Someone who can architect the position itself.

### Decision 2 — Raise prices, hold, or restructure the offers?

**Recommendation: restructure the offers first, then raise prices on the new structure.**

Raising prices on your current offering (Q77 — bespoke, fails all three real-product tests) doesn't survive sales conversations, because there's no defensible reason for the price.

Restructure first: take your one named offering (Q79 — the asset you've actually built) and make it teachable, valuable, repeatable. Then re-price it on the basis of the verbatim customer sentences you've gathered in the Sprint.

### Decision 3 — Where do I cut vs double down this quarter?

**Recommendation: cut new ad spend, cut new hires, cut new positioning experiments. Double down on three things only.**

- The 30 customer calls.
- Restructuring the one named offering.
- Paying yourself ₹2L/month for three months as the discipline anchor that forces every other decision to align.

The first month you can't make payroll *and* pay yourself ₹2L will be the first month you're allowed to change something structural about the business. That single constraint will teach you more about your operating reality than any analytics dashboard.

## Appendix B: Glossary

**8 Forces:** The eight depth dimensions of the bootcamp curriculum.
- *Identity (1):* who you serve and what business you're actually in.
- *X-Factor (2):* the cost you pay that competitors refuse to.
- *Marketing (3):* the position, the message, the channel.
- *Sales (4):* turning interest into a paying customer.
- *Financial (5):* the honesty of your numbers.
- *Optimisation (6):* working through the real bottleneck, not around it.
- *Scale (7):* the asset that exists without you.
- *Owner Energy (8):* what you've subtracted to make the important few possible.

**The 9 Lies:** Common founder beliefs the bootcamp surfaces and breaks. Identity Trap, X-Factor Hunt, Marketing Lie, Sales Lie, Conversion Lie, Profit Lie, Growth Lie, Freedom Lie, Death Spiral.

**The 7 Founder Archetypes:** Cross-force patterns common to Indian SME founders. The Grinder, The Inheritor, The Migrator (here called *The Ad-Funded Founder* for plain language), The Name (The Personal Brand), The Rainmaker's Firm (The Agency Model), The Institution, The Architect.

**Replacement wage (Bus Test):** What the market would pay to do your job, end-to-end. Used to correct the real margin — true profit = stated profit minus the implicit subsidy of paying yourself less than market.

**Layer-3 Bleeder:** Running paid distribution (ads, reels, agencies) without a clear position underneath. The single most expensive recoverable mistake in Indian SME marketing.

**Customer-Voice Sprint:** A 90-day instrument — 30 customer interviews → 5 verbatim sentences → 1 chosen position. The rule: a position written without customer evidence won't survive its first pricing objection.
